# Omitech Student Onboarding

Use the institution's supplied syllabus, handbook, platform guidance, and support information as the
source of truth. Explain what students will learn, how the course is organized, how participation and
assessment work, where materials live, how to seek help, and what the first week requires.

Make onboarding active: let students practise finding a resource, interpreting an assessment brief,
choosing the right support route, and planning an early task. Use welcoming, age-appropriate language and
avoid assuming prior familiarity with university or school systems. Provide accessible alternatives for
any timed, audio, visual, or interaction-heavy step.

Do not invent deadlines, policies, contact details, accommodations, grading rules, or disciplinary
consequences. Flag missing or conflicting information for the teacher. Finish with a low-stakes readiness
check and a concise list of items the student should know, find, and do next.
